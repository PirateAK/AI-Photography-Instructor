## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT

**Welcome**

Every camera has a ceiling: the span between the brightest detail it can hold and the darkest it can separate. Scenes don't care. When the scene's span exceeds the sensor's, something gets lost — and this lesson is about choosing, deliberately, what gets lost.

**What dynamic range actually is**

Dynamic range is a ratio: the brightest detail your sensor can record before it clips, against the darkest shadow where real information still exists. Expose for the highlights and shadows fall to black; expose for the shadows and highlights blow to white. RAW files buy latitude — the Intermediate RAW lesson showed the recovery — but latitude is not infinite, and JPEG shoots spend it faster.

**The four strategies for high-contrast scenes**

One: sacrifice the shadows — expose for highlights, let the darks go graphic. Two: sacrifice the highlights — expose for shadows and accept blown windows as a look. Three: add light — flash or a reflector lifts the shadows so the span fits. Four: bracket — several exposures for blending later. Each is a legitimate style; the mistake is letting the camera choose by accident.

**Reading the histogram like a forecast**

The histogram is the dynamic-range report. A shape hugging the right wall means clipped highlights no edit recovers; a left wall pile means crushed blacks. On a high-contrast scene the shape spans both walls — the forecast that tells you to pick a strategy before you shoot, not after.

**Demonstration: one high-contrast scene, three approaches**

A dark interior with a bright window. Frame one: metered normally — window blows, room holds. Frame two: exposed for the window — the room goes moody and dark, details gone by design. Frame three: bracketed at two stops apart for blending. Three honest files, three different images, one deliberate decision each.

**Learner practice**

Photograph a high-contrast scene three ways — protect highlights, protect shadows, bracket — and write down what each version sacrificed. The sacrifice list is the lesson.

**Recap**

When the scene's span exceeds the sensor's, choose what gets lost: shadows, highlights, added light, or brackets. The histogram is the forecast. Decide before the shutter — not in the edit, when it's too late.

## 6. LEARNER PRACTICE

Photograph one high-contrast scene — a room with a bright window, or a shaded foreground under a bright sky — three ways:

1. Exposed to protect highlights.
2. Exposed to protect shadows.
3. Bracketed two stops apart for blending.

For each version write down what was sacrificed. Then choose which version best serves the photograph you wanted — and whether blending is worth the extra workflow for this scene.

If you're using a phone: the phone's auto HDR does this blending invisibly. Turn HDR off (if possible), take the single frames, and see the problem the phone has been solving for you.

## 7. RECAP

Dynamic range is the span between held highlights and separated shadows — and high-contrast scenes exceed it. Choose the sacrifice: shadows as graphic, highlights as look, added light to fit, or brackets to blend. The histogram is the forecast; read it before the shutter.
