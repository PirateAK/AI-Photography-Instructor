Class: Advanced

## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT

**Welcome**

Everything through the Intermediate class — exposure, focus, light, composition — transfers to every genre. What changes by genre is the PRIORITY ORDER and the working conditions. This lesson maps six fields and demonstrates one in depth.

**The genre map: six fields, six demands**

Wildlife: long lenses, patient fieldcraft, and shutter priority at distance. Sports: continuous focus, anticipation, and the willingness to discard ninety frames. Architecture: verticals, perspective control, and interior light balance. Macro: thin depth of field pushed to its limit, and tripod discipline. Events: consistency under pressure — the same correct exposure, scene after scene, with no reshoots. Food: controlled light, restrained styling, and color honesty. Notice each one is the Intermediate class with a different priority order.

**The transfer method: shared skills, changed priorities**

The method for entering any genre: take the skills you own, then reorder their priorities for the genre's demands. Wildlife demands shutter first and reach second. Architecture demands verticals first and exposure blending second. Food demands color accuracy first and styling second. The skills don't change — the order does. In this app, the style selector makes that literal: each genre's style loads its own recipe and priorities, so you can see exactly how the order shifts.

**Demonstration: one genre in depth (wildlife)**

Wildlife as the worked example. Equipment: the longest lens available, stabilization, and patience as the primary accessory. Settings: shutter priority with a floor near 1/1000 for active animals, continuous autofocus with a zone, ISO allowed to climb — noise beats blur. Composition: eye-level with the animal, space in the frame in the direction of movement. Working conditions: distance you cannot close, subjects that will not repeat, and ethics — the animal's wellbeing outranks the photograph, always. Load the Wildlife style in the simulator and you'll see this priority order as the recipe the coach uses to judge those photos.

**Choosing your genre(s)**

Choose by honest questions. What do you photograph when nobody assigns it? What conditions can you sustain — early mornings, crowds, long stillness? Genres are not fences: skills cross-pollinate, and most working photographers hold two or three. Pick one to go deep on, and let the others borrow.

**Learner practice**

Choose one genre and plan a shoot for it: the equipment, the priority order, the working conditions, and one photograph you'll try to make. Execute, then review against the genre's demands, not just your general standards.

**Recap**

Skills are shared; genres specialize by priority order. Wildlife wants shutter and patience, sports wants anticipation, architecture wants verticals, macro wants depth discipline, events want consistency, food wants color honesty. Load the genre's style, reorder your priorities, and go deep on one.

## 6. LEARNER PRACTICE

Choose ONE genre from the map and plan a shoot:

1. Write its priority order — which skill leads, which supports, which waits.
2. List the equipment honestly: what you have, what it truly requires, what can wait.
3. Shoot one session in that genre.
4. Review against the genre's demands — for wildlife, sharpness at distance; for events, consistency; for food, color honesty.

Load the matching style in the app before and after: compare the coach's read against the genre's priorities.

If you're using a phone: genres still apply — a phone shoots events and food and street credibly, and each genre's priority order remains the same even where the dials are hidden.

## 7. RECAP

Skills are shared; genres specialize by priority order and working conditions. Wildlife, sports, architecture, macro, events, food — each reorders the same Intermediate skills. The app's style selector makes the reordering visible. Choose one genre, go deep, and let the others borrow.

## 8. ACCURACY CHECK

- Genre priority orders are conventions of the craft, not rules; present them as starting priorities.
- Wildlife ethics: the animal's welfare outranks the photograph; no baiting, flushing, or habitat disturbance for the demonstration.
- Sports and event claims must match the demonstrated conditions; do not promise specific hit rates.
- The app's genre styles are teaching recipes, not prescriptions — present them as priority orders, verified against the styles' actual contents.
- Any specialized equipment shown must be identified honestly, including what it does NOT do.
