## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT

**Welcome**

The Intermediate class treated depth of field and dynamic range as limits to work within. This lesson is about the scenes that blow past those limits — and the three techniques that combine multiple exposures to beat them: panorama for framing, focus stacking for depth, and HDR for brightness.

**Three limits, three techniques**

Framing limit: the scene is wider than your widest lens — or you need more resolution than one frame holds. Panorama: several overlapping frames stitched into one larger image. Focus limit: the depth of field needed exceeds what any single aperture delivers — focus stacking shoots the same frame at stepped focus distances and blends only the sharp parts. Exposure limit: the dynamic range exceeds one frame — HDR brackets and blends, exactly the advanced exposure lesson's fourth strategy executed. Same discipline, three limits.

**The capture rules that make combining possible**

Whichever technique, the capture discipline is the same: the frames must ALIGN. Lock the camera on a tripod (panorama can pivot around the lens's nodal point if you're serious; the rest of us overlap generously). Keep exposure, focus plan, and framing consistent across frames — change only the variable being stacked. Overlap by a third. And don't change the lighting between frames, or the blend will show seams no software can hide.

**Demonstration: focus stacking end to end**

A macro subject — a watch mechanism — where even f/16 holds only a sliver of sharpness. Capture: camera locked, focus stepped from the nearest point to the farthest in even increments, eight frames, nothing else touched. Alignment: the stacking software registers the frames and builds a depth map. Blend: only the sharp region of each frame survives into the composite. Review: the finished image holds the whole mechanism sharp — impossible in a single frame at any aperture. And check the honesty: no ghosting, no haloed edges, no misaligned seams.

**Panorama and HDR: the same discipline, other limits**

Panorama: pivot steadily with a third overlap, keep exposure and white balance locked across frames, and let the stitching software build a frame wider than any lens. HDR: the bracketed pair from the advanced exposure lesson, blended so highlights AND shadows survive. Both live and die by the capture rules.

**When NOT to combine**

Combining has costs: moving subjects ghost, wind moves foliage between frames, and triple the captures triples the workflow. If one frame can honestly make the photograph — wider lens, stopped down, exposed well — one frame IS the better photograph. The techniques rescue limits; they don't replace craft.

**Learner practice**

Choose ONE technique that solves a real limit you hit this week, capture a compliant set, combine it, and review the result honestly for seams, ghosts, and halos.

**Recap**

Panorama beats framing limits, focus stacking beats depth limits, HDR beats brightness limits — and all three demand aligned captures: locked camera, consistent settings, generous overlap. Combine when the limit is real; shoot one frame when it isn't.

## 6. LEARNER PRACTICE

Choose the technique that solves a limit you actually hit:

1. Panorama: a scene wider than your lens — locked exposure, 30% overlap, steady pivot.
2. Focus stack: a close subject — stepped focus, locked everything else, even increments.
3. HDR: a high-contrast scene — bracket two stops apart.

Combine in software, then review honestly: seams, ghosting, halos. Write down what the combined image achieved that the single frame could not — and whether the extra workflow was worth it.

If you're using a phone: panorama is built in — shoot one and note how the phone handles the alignment. Focus stacking and manual HDR remain camera techniques; the review skills transfer.

## 7. RECAP

Three limits, three combinations: panorama for framing, focus stacking for depth, HDR for brightness. Capture rules make it work — locked camera, consistent settings, generous overlap, one variable changed. Combine when the limit is real; the best photograph is still sometimes one frame.

## 8. ACCURACY CHECK

- Software steps vary across stacking, stitching, and blending tools; demonstrate only the tool filmed.
- Moving subjects ghost in stacks and HDR — demonstrate static subjects or show the failure honestly.
- The focus-step increments must be shown or stated; uneven steps create blend failures.
- Do not overclaim resolution gains from panorama without noting the stitching trade-offs.
- Verify every before/after claim against the actual source frames and composite.
