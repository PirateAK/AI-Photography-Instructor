## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT

**Welcome**

Your screen emits light; paper reflects it. That single difference explains every disappointment between the edited image and the printed one — and this lesson is about managing it deliberately, not being surprised by it.

**Why print disagrees: light through ink vs light from glass**

A screen builds colors from glowing pixels, with a brightness range papers cannot reach: screen "black" is a lit black; paper "black" is ink reflecting whatever light falls on it. Screens show vivid, luminous color; print renders darker, denser, and smaller-ranged. Neither is wrong — they are different media, and the photographer's job is translating between them.

**Color spaces: sRGB, Adobe RGB, and the printer's smaller box**

A color space is the box of colors a device can express. sRGB is the small universal box — the web's language. Adobe RGB is wider — greens and cyans the web rarely shows. A printer's space is its own box, usually narrower than either in places, occasionally wider in a few. The working rule: shoot and edit with headroom (RAW, or a wide space if you know why), deliver in the space the destination needs — and never let the web shrink a wide-space file silently.

**Soft proofing: rehearsing the print**

Soft proofing asks the editing software to simulate, on screen, what the printer's space will do to your image — using the printer and paper's profile. Colors the paper can't reach get flagged; you adjust knowing exactly which direction paper will pull. It's a rehearsal for the print, and the single most useful habit in this lesson: proof, adjust, proof again, then export for THAT printer and THAT paper.

**Demonstration: one file to a finished print**

A saturated sunset file. On screen: luminous. Soft-proofed for matte paper: the bright oranges desaturate and the deep blues lift — the proof shows the paper's smaller box honestly. Adjustments: gentle saturation and a slight brightness lift to compensate for matte's density — restrained, watching the proof. Export at print resolution (300 pixels per inch at the print size as a working standard), as a copy, original untouched. The print arrives close to the proof — close, not identical, and that's the craft's honest ceiling.

**The two print rules that matter most**

One: soft proof before every print — it converts surprise into decision. Two: keep the original untouched; print exports are copies, per the workflow lesson. Everything else — profiles, ppi, paper choice — is detail orbiting these two habits.

**Learner practice**

Prepare one photograph for print: soft proof for a specific paper, adjust against the proof, export at print size, and compare the print with the screen. Write down what changed and whether the proof predicted it.

**Recap**

Screens glow; paper reflects — the disagreement is physical. Color spaces are boxes, and delivery means the destination's box. Soft proofing rehearses the print so surprises become decisions. Export at print size, as a copy, and let the original stay sacred.

## 6. LEARNER PRACTICE

Take one photograph through the print-preparation process:

1. Choose the print size and paper (even for a print service — ask for their profile if offered).
2. Soft proof against that paper; note which colors shift.
3. Adjust against the proof — restrained, as always.
4. Export a print-size copy as a separate file; original untouched.
5. Print it (or order the print) and compare honestly against the screen. Write down what the proof predicted and what it missed.

If you're using a phone: the lesson's first half is the takeaway — phones over-brighten and over-saturate. Compare a phone-edited photo printed via a service against the screen, and you'll see why soft proofing exists.

## 7. RECAP

Screens emit; paper reflects — print will always be a translation. Color spaces are boxes; deliver in the destination's box. Soft proofing rehearses the print and turns surprise into decision. Export at print size, as a copy — and the two rules that matter: proof first, original untouched.

## 8. ACCURACY CHECK

- Color profiles, soft proofing availability, and print behavior vary across software, printers, and papers; demonstrate only the setup filmed.
- Do not promise print-screen matches — present soft proofing as reducing, not eliminating, surprise.
- PPI/resolution guidance varies with print size and viewing distance; present 300 ppi as a working standard, not a law.
- Monitor calibration exists on a spectrum from uncalibrated to hardware-calibrated; be honest about where the demonstration setup sits.
- Verify the physical print comparison shown reflects the stated paper and profile.
