## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT

**Welcome**

Global edits set the photograph's foundation; finishing is the surgical layer — light placed locally, distractions removed, color guided along the eye's path. The skill ceiling isn't software knowledge. It's judgment: every adjustment must survive the question, "does this serve the photograph, or announce itself?"

**The ethics baseline: believable and honest**

Two lines define honest finishing. Believable: the result must look like something the camera could have seen — no plastic skin, no impossible physics, no halos. Honest: the image must not deceive about what happened — news and documentary work removes dust and adjusts exposure, and does not remove the people or facts that were there. Personal and commercial work has more latitude, but the believable line holds everywhere. When in doubt, the photograph's stated purpose sets the rules.

**Local adjustments: light where the story needs it**

Global edits treat every pixel equally; local tools — brushes, gradients, radial masks — direct attention. Darken the edges slightly and the eye stays in the frame. Lift the subject's face a quarter stop and the portrait gains presence no one names. Dodge and burn — lightening and darkening small regions — is a century old and still the most powerful finishing move: it shapes the viewer's journey through the frame.

**Distraction removal: subtracting the accidental**

Every frame contains accidents: a sensor spot, a stray wrapper, a bright exit sign. Removal tools heal and clone, and the rule is subtracting the ACCIDENTAL, not the inconvenient. A litter wrapper in a landscape is an accident — remove it. A person who was genuinely part of the scene is a fact — removing them is a statement you'd better intend. If removal requires inventing content that changes what happened, that's beyond finishing and into fabrication — a different discussion, and a different photograph.

**Targeted color: shaping the eye's path**

Global color was the Intermediate lesson; targeted color is finer: warm the subject slightly against a cooler background, calm one loud hue, enrich another. The eye follows warmth and saturation — guide it the way you guided light with dodge and burn. One test governs all of it: view the image small. If the finish looks like a finish at thumbnail size, it's too much.

**Demonstration: one image, finished**

One RAW file, already globally edited. Step one: dust spots and a stray wrapper removed — the accidents. Step two: a soft gradient darkening the corners a touch — the eye held in frame. Step three: the subject's face lifted a quarter stop; the background cooled slightly — the path shaped. Before and after: the finished version looks like the same photograph on its best day. Nothing announces itself.

**Learner practice**

Finish one globally-edited image with three local moves — a dodge, a burn, and one targeted color change — plus one distraction removal. Then view it small, and view it beside the original. Write the purpose of each move.

**Recap**

Finishing is surgical: light placed where the story needs it, accidents subtracted, color guiding the eye. The lines are believable and honest. And the final test is the thumbnail — if the finish announces itself, it's too much.

## 6. LEARNER PRACTICE

Finish one globally-edited photograph:

1. Remove the accidents — sensor spots, stray objects that were never part of the scene.
2. Dodge and burn: one region lightened, one darkened, both serving the eye's path.
3. One targeted color move: warmth or calm, guided by the story.
4. View the result small, beside the original. Write the purpose of each move — and undo any move you can't justify.

If you're using a phone: local edits exist in mobile editors, and the thumbnail test matters even more on the device the photo will mostly be seen on.

## 7. RECAP

Finishing is surgical: local light, accident subtraction, and targeted color shape the eye's journey. The lines are believable and honest — subtract the accidental, not the factual. The final test is the thumbnail: a finish that announces itself is a finish too far.

## 8. ACCURACY CHECK

- Software tools vary (masks, healing, cloning, AI-assisted removal); demonstrate only the tool filmed and name it honestly.
- The believable/honest line is presented as the lesson's position — documentary and news standards are stricter; state that if the context is journalism.
- AI-assisted removal/expansion features vary and can fabricate content; if shown, label the behavior explicitly.
- Before/after claims must be verifiable in the screen recording; no selective comparisons.
- Skin retouching, if mentioned, follows the same believable line — texture preserved, not erased.
