## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT
Welcome back to the final lesson of our beginner photography series. Today we are tackling the digital darkroom: basic photo organization and editing. 

If you have been following along, you know how to set your exposure, lock in your focus, and capture incredible moments in the field. But here is a secret every experienced photographer knows: the shot isn't finished when the shutter closes. What you do *after* the shoot is just as important as what you do while you are taking pictures. And I promise, you do not need an expensive computer science degree to keep your photos safe and looking their absolute best. Here is your first small win for today: just realizing that editing is not "cheating"—it is simply finishing the creative process you started in the field.

Let us start with the big picture. Every photographer, from a smartphone hobbyist in Alaska to a wildlife pro, follows the exact same five-step workflow: import, organize, select, edit, and export. Even when the buttons and apps change, the steps stay the same. 

Step one is import. That just means getting your photos off your camera card and onto a reliable device or hard drive. 

Step two is organize. The golden rule here is simple: findable beats clever. Do not waste time making dozens of complex folders. Just use a folder per project or date, like "2024-10-15-AnchoragePark". You want to find your files in three clicks or less. Here is a small win you can try: set up just one single folder on your desktop today for your upcoming weekend walk.

Step three is select. This is where we act as our own toughest critic. You will look at your batch and choose with purpose based on five criteria: focus, expression, composition, light, and whether the photo actually says what you intended it to say. Swipe away the misfires—the blurry shots, the accidental floor pictures, the eyes-closed blinks. Be ruthless with the delete button, or at least hide them out of sight. 

Step four is edit, and this is where the magic happens. Here is our golden rule of editing: edit with a light touch. Brightness, contrast, and color can lift a photo and make it sing, while strong, heavy-handed changes push an image until it looks fake and unnatural. Think of editing like seasoning a meal. A pinch of salt brings out the flavor; dumping the whole container ruins the dinner. 

Let us break down those three simple edit adjustments. First, brightness: this is how light or dark your overall image appears. Second, contrast: the difference between your bright areas and your dark areas, which adds punch and depth. Third, color, which we control through white balance—making sure white things look white instead of weirdly blue or yellow. 

While you make these tiny adjustments, you will want a trusty co-pilot: the histogram. A histogram is simply a little graph on your screen that shows you the brightness levels of your photo from pure black on the left to pure white on the right. It keeps you honest so you do not accidentally blast your bright highlights into pure white or crush your dark shadows into solid black mud. 

Here is the most important rule of the digital age: protect your originals. Always edit in a way that leaves your original file completely untouched, or save your edited versions as brand-new, separate copies. If you make a mess of an edit, you can always go right back to the pristine camera original. 

Step five is export. That means saving a final, finished copy in a standard format—like a JPEG—ready to share with friends, family, or print out for your wall. 

You have built incredible skills in this course. Take a deep breath, trust your eye, and let us get those photos looking their absolute best.

## 6. LEARNER PRACTICE
Run the whole workflow in the app: pick a small set of photos into your gallery (import), swipe away the misfires and blurry shots (organize), load your top three into the coach and use its scores and notes to choose your absolute favorite (select—and say why you picked it out loud). Then on that winning photo, preview a restrained edit: nudge your exposure compensation slider slightly for brightness, try a picture style for contrast, and shift your white balance for color. Watch your histogram to keep your highlights and shadows honest, remembering that your gallery original never changes. When it looks right, recreate those same small moves in your editing app and export a separate final copy, leaving your original safely untouched.

## 7. RECAP
- **Protect your originals:** Always keep your raw camera files untouched by saving edits as separate new copies.
- **Keep organization simple:** Remember that findable beats clever—use simple date or project folders.
- **Select with purpose:** Choose your best shots based on focus, expression, composition, light, and intent while ruthlessly culling misfires.
- **Edit with a light touch:** Treat adjustments like seasoning; subtle tweaks to brightness, contrast, and color look natural and professional.
- **Use the histogram:** Let the brightness graph keep your highlights and shadows safe from clipping.
