# Nikon School Online Lesson 1 - Setting up a Nikon Z body right

## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT
Welcome back. Today we are diving straight into setting up your Nikon Z body the right way—whether you are shooting with a Z50, a Z6, or a Z8. I promise, by the end of this lesson, you will stop fighting your camera's default menus and start making the gear work for you. 

When you first unbox a mirrorless camera, the menus can feel overwhelming. There are hundreds of options, and frankly, 80 percent of them you will rarely touch. Our goal today is to focus on the 20 percent that actually speed up your workflow and help you capture the shot before the moment passes. 

Let's start right at the top with your menu system. Grab your camera or look at the simulator on your screen. Hit the Menu button on the back left. You will see a series of icons down the left side: the playback menu, the photo shooting menu, the custom settings menu, the setup menu, the retouch menu, and my menu. 

The custom settings menu—represented by the pencil icon—is where the real magic happens. This is where we tell the camera how to behave. Let's make our first tweak right here. Scroll down to custom setting d9, which is your exposure preview. By default, some Z bodies might have this set to off in certain manual flash modes, but we want it on. Exposure preview means what you see in the electronic viewfinder or on the rear screen is a live simulation of your final exposure. If you dial your shutter speed up to one-thousandth of a second and your image goes dark on screen, you know immediately your shot is underexposed. That real-time feedback is one of the greatest superpowers of shooting mirrorless.

Next, let's talk about image review. That is the setting that pops your photo up on the rear screen the second you press the shutter button. Turn it off. I know, I know—it feels safe to check your work. But if the screen lights up for two seconds after every single shot, you lose precious seconds looking backward instead of watching the scene unfold in front of you. Trust your electronic viewfinder. If you want to review a shot, press the play button manually. Otherwise, keep your eye on the glass.

Now, let's move on to the holy grail of camera customization: back-button autofocus. By default, your camera focuses every time you press the shutter button halfway down. That works fine until a branch sways in front of your subject, or you want to recompose a portrait. When the shutter button does both focusing and shooting, the camera gets confused and tries to refocus every time you trip the shutter. 

Let's fix that right now. Head into the custom settings menu, find the controls section—labeled with the letter 'f' on most Z bodies—and look for custom setting f2, which is your custom controls assignment. Scroll down until you find the AF-ON button on the back right of your camera body. We are going to assign autofocus exclusively to that button under your thumb. Then, we are going to go into the shutter release button settings and turn off autofocus activation there. 

Let's trace what happens when you shoot this way. Your right thumb rests on the AF-ON button. You press it once to lock focus on your subject's eye. Once focus is locked, you can lift your thumb off the button. The focus stays locked right there. Now, your index finger is free to rest lightly on the shutter button, waiting for the exact right moment to click. You can recompose your shot, wait for a smile, or shoot a burst of three frames at eight frames per second, and the camera will never hunt for focus again. It takes about ten minutes of practice to retrain your muscle memory, but once it clicks, you will never go back to standard shutter-button focusing.

While we are in the custom controls menu, let's look at the sub-selector center click—that is the little joystick right under your thumb. I like to program the center click of that joystick to instantly jump my focus point back to the center of the frame. If you have been tracking a bird flying off to the top right corner and need to snap back to a dead-center subject instantly, one click of that joystick does it. No frantic scrolling required.

Now that our core controls are locked in, let's tackle the i-menu. The i-menu is your quick-access dashboard. When you are looking through the viewfinder or at the rear screen, you press the little "i" button in the bottom right corner, and a grid of twelve settings pops up. Out of the box, Nikon gives you a solid default layout, but it probably includes things you rarely change—like D-Lighting or touch screen controls. Let's customize it so your most critical settings are right there under your thumb.

Press your i-button right now and let's edit the layout. If you shoot landscapes or architecture, you want electronic front-curtain shutter or silent photography right there in slot one. If you shoot fast-moving action, you want your AF-area mode—say, Wide-area AF person tracking—in slot one. Let's put image quality, white balance, metering mode, and silent photography into the top row. Now, when the light changes rapidly from open shade to bright sun, you do not have to dive into a deep menu system. You press the i-button, tap your screen or use the dial, and change your white balance from auto to direct sunlight in less than two seconds. 

Let's save these settings so you never have to repeat the process. On advanced bodies like the Z6 and Z8, you have user assignment modes right on the top dial—U1, U2, and U3. Once you have your menus optimized, your back-button autofocus set up, and your i-menu customized for everyday shooting, go to your setup menu and select "Save user settings." Assign it to U1. Now, set up a secondary profile on U2 for low-light tripod work with long exposures, manual focus, and a two-second timer. Turn the dial to U1 for street walkabouts, and switch to U2 the second you set up your tripod at dusk. Your camera instantly reconfigures every custom button and menu choice in a fraction of a second.

Take a deep breath. Setting up a camera body this way feels like learning a new language for the first time, but every single adjustment we made today is designed to eliminate friction between your creative vision and the final image. You are no longer fighting the factory defaults. You are running a machine tailored specifically to how you shoot. 

In our next lesson, we will take this custom-built machine out into the field and put our exposure triangle to the test. For now, take a few minutes to walk through your menus, lock in that back-button focus, and make the i-menu your own. I will see you in the next lesson.

## 7. RECAP
- **Menu Customization:** Enable exposure preview (d9) for real-time visual feedback of your exposure settings.
- **Streamlined Workflow:** Turn off image review to keep your eye on the scene and avoid missing fleeting moments.
- **Back-Button Autofocus:** Separate focusing from the shutter release by assigning AF-ON to your thumb for total control over recomposing.
- **Custom i-Menu:** Populate your quick-access dashboard with the four tools you use most often.

## 8. ACCURACY CHECK
- **Question:** Which custom setting controls the assignment of camera buttons like the AF-ON button on Nikon Z bodies?
- **Answer:** Custom setting f2 (Custom controls assignment).

- **Question:** Why is turning off image review recommended for fast-paced shooting?
- **Answer:** It prevents the rear screen from lighting up after every shot, keeping your eye in the viewfinder so you don't miss action unfolding in front of you.

- **Question:** What is the primary benefit of assigning autofocus to the back AF-ON button instead of a half-press of the shutter?
- **Answer:** It allows you to lock focus once and freely recompose your shot or fire multiple frames without the camera hunting for focus again.
