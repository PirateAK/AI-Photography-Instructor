# Online Workshops Lesson 3 of 3 - Choosing your next workshop

## 1. LEARNING OUTCOME

## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT
Welcome back to our workshop series. Today we are tackling a really common frustration: spending your hard-earned money on a photography workshop, sitting through every minute, and realizing a week later that your photos haven't actually gotten any better. 

I’ve been there. You see a gorgeous landscape ad or a moody street photography class online, you hit buy, and you spend a weekend learning stuff you already half-know. It feels fun in the moment, but it doesn't move the needle. Today, we're going to fix that. By the end of this lesson, you'll know how to audit your own portfolio and data so you can pick workshops that surgically fix your actual weak spots instead of just repeating what you're already good at.

Let’s start with a hard truth. As photographers, we love shooting what we're comfortable with. If you love bright, airy portraits, you probably have your ISO at 100, your aperture at f/2.0, and you're making gorgeous shots in open shade. But what happens when a client asks you to shoot a dimly lit corporate event at ISO 6400 with a shutter speed of 1/60th to handhold a 70-200mm lens? Suddenly, your comfort zone evaporates. 

Auditing your weaknesses means looking past the three or four hero shots you post on social media and looking at the hundreds of frames that didn't make the cut. Let's look at how to do this right inside our app. 

Pull up your Progress screen on your device right now. Go ahead, I'll wait. Look at your saved session logs from the last month. We aren't looking at the art right now; we're looking at the data. 

Check your exposure logs. Do you see a recurring pattern where 30 percent of your low-light shots are blurry because your shutter speed dropped below 1/125th? Or look at your histogram data—that little graph showing the tonal distribution from shadows on the left to highlights on the right. Are your outdoor shots constantly clipping the highlights on the far right because you're struggling to balance bright sunlight at f/1.4? 

Write down the two most common failures you see in that data. Is it missed focus on moving subjects? Is it muddy shadows because you're afraid of pushing your ISO past 800? Or maybe it's psychological—are you avoiding street photography because you're terrified of interacting with strangers? 

Once you name the monster, you can kill it. This is your filter for choosing your next workshop. 

Never buy a workshop based on how pretty the promotional video looks. Buy a workshop based on a specific, measurable gap in your data. If your Progress screen tells you that 40 percent of your macro shots are soft because you don't understand depth of field at close range, do not sign up for a general composition masterclass. Sign up for a technical lighting and focus-stacking workshop. 

When you read a workshop syllabus, look for the specific module that scares you a little bit. If a description of flash-sync speeds or manual white-balance calibration makes your stomach drop, that is the exact workshop you need to take. Growth doesn't live in the classes where the instructor confirms what you already do. Growth lives in the uncomfortable thirty minutes where you finally figure out why your flash photos look orange and flat.

Let’s talk about vetting the instructor, too. Look for teachers who explain the "why" behind the settings, not just the "what." A good instructor tells you, "We are setting our shutter speed to 1/500th at f/4 to freeze this runner while keeping the background softly blurred." A great instructor explains what happens to your image noise when you underexpose by one stop and try to pull the shadows back up in editing software later. 

You don't need five workshops a year. You need one targeted workshop that fixes the exact technical mistake you made three times last Tuesday. 

Before we wrap up, I want to give you a quick assignment to put this into practice today. Take five minutes with your app. Use the Progress screen to find your single weakest recurring issue across your last ten saved sessions. Is it focus? Is it exposure? Is it motion blur? Once you find that one metric, use it as your search keyword to hunt down your next online workshop. 

Thank you for joining me for this series. Keep shooting, keep analyzing your data, and remember that every mistake in your camera roll is just a free lesson waiting to be learned. See you next time.

Review your last ten saved shooting sessions and identify your single weakest recurring issue (such as persistent motion blur, consistent underexposure, or missed focus). Write down that specific technical problem, and use it as the mandatory filter when searching for your next external workshop.

## 7. RECAP
- Don't buy workshops based on pretty marketing; buy them to fix specific, measurable gaps in your data.
- Use your app's Progress screen and exposure logs to find your true recurring weak spots instead of relying on memory.
- Look past your favorite "hero" shots and analyze the outtakes and failed frames.
- Choose instructors who explain the technical "why" behind settings rather than just general theory.
- Lean into the topics that make you slightly uncomfortable—that's where real skill growth happens.
