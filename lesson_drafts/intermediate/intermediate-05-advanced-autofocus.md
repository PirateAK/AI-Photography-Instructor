# Intermediate Lesson 5 — Advanced Autofocus Techniques

Class: Intermediate
Prerequisite: Beginner lesson on focus and autofocus

## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT

**Welcome**

Autofocus is a partnership. The camera does the micro-adjustments far faster than your fingers could — but you decide WHAT it should lock onto. Beginner class taught focus placement; this lesson teaches the camera's half of the partnership: modes and areas.

**Two modes, two jobs: single and continuous**

Single autofocus — one letter or another depending on your brand — locks focus once, when you half-press. It's the right tool for stationary subjects: precise, deliberate, done. Continuous autofocus keeps re-focusing for as long as the shutter is half-pressed, chasing your subject as it moves. Stationary subject, single mode. Moving subject, continuous. Almost every focus problem starts with the wrong one of these two.

**Focus areas: point, zone, and wide**

Within each mode, you choose WHERE the camera looks. A single point is the scalpel: precise, but it must be placed exactly. A zone covers a small region and lets the camera pick within it. Wide or automatic area hands the choice to the camera entirely — convenient, and occasionally wrong about what matters. The wider the area, the more the camera decides; the tighter, the more you do.

**Demonstration: stationary subject, then a walker**

First, a stationary subject in single mode with a small point placed on the detail that matters — a book title, an eye. Confirm focus, shoot, and the frame is sharp exactly where intended. Now a person walking toward the camera: single mode would lock once and the subject walks out of focus. Continuous mode with a zone around the subject keeps re-locking frame after frame. Same scene, different partnership terms.

**Tracking and subject detection: what modern cameras add**

Newer cameras add tracking — the camera follows a chosen subject across the frame — and subject detection that recognizes eyes, faces, or animals automatically. These features are genuinely useful and genuinely model-dependent: what one camera does flawlessly, another attempts. Know what YOUR camera offers before trusting it with an unrepeatable moment.

**Reviewing for sharpness: keep, retry, adjust**

With movement, not every frame lands sharp — professionals rate and cull too. Review the series enlarged, sort into keep and missed, and if too many missed, adjust: wider zone, faster shutter, or a different approach. The review loop is the skill.

**Learner practice**

Photograph a moving person or object in continuous mode. Review enlarged, count your sharp frames, and change one variable — area size or mode — if the hit rate disappoints. Then say what you changed and why.

**Recap**

Single mode for stationary, continuous for movement. Point areas give control; wide areas give speed at the cost of say. Tracking and subject detection are powerful but model-dependent. And the review loop — shoot, enlarge, sort, adjust — is where autofocus skill actually forms.

## 6. LEARNER PRACTICE

Photograph a moving person or object in continuous autofocus:

1. Choose a focus area you think suits the subject's size and speed.
2. Shoot a short series as the subject moves toward or across you.
3. Review enlarged: count sharp frames versus missed.
4. Change ONE variable — area size, or single vs continuous — and shoot another series. Compare hit rates.

Write down which setup produced the better hit rate and why you believe it helped.

If you're using a phone: tap to focus on the moving subject and take a burst if your phone offers one. Review which frames the phone kept sharp — the review loop is identical even when the controls are hidden.

## 7. RECAP

Single autofocus locks once — for still subjects. Continuous chases — for movement. Tight areas give you control; wide areas give the camera the say. Tracking and subject detection are modern and model-dependent. Shoot, enlarge, sort, adjust — the review loop is the skill.

## 8. ACCURACY CHECK

- Mode and area names differ substantially across brands and models; show or state only the names used on the camera filmed, presenting others as examples.
- Subject-detection features (eye, face, animal, vehicle) vary by model and generation; do not promise detection behavior on cameras where it is unverified.
- Continuous-autofocus hit rates depend on lens, light, subject contrast, and speed; do not guarantee specific results.
- If back-button or customized focus assignment is mentioned, present it as an option available on many but not all cameras, configured in menus that vary by model.
- Verify every stated mode/area against the camera's actual display before publishing.
