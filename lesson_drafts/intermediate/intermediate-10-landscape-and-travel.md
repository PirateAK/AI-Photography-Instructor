# Intermediate Lesson 10 — Landscape and Travel Photography

Class: Intermediate
Prerequisite: Beginner lessons on composition, lighting, exposure, and lenses

## 1. LEARNING OUTCOME

By the end of this lesson, learners can plan a location shoot and make photographs that communicate a sense of place — using varied framings rather than many similar frames.

## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT

**Welcome**

Landscape and travel photography share one truth: you can't reposition the mountain. The craft is in choosing where you stand, when you're there, and how many different stories you collect before you leave.

**Scouting and timing: light makes the appointment**

Light makes the appointment; the scenery just attends. The same viewpoint at noon and at golden hour produces two different photographs. Use maps and sun-position apps to know where light will come from, arrive early enough to walk the location, and have a fallback for flat light: details, textures, and tighter scenes survive overcast skies better than grand vistas do.

**The three-shot habit: wide, detail, scale**

Wherever you go, collect three kinds of frame. The wide view: the whole place, foreground anchored, horizon placed with intent. The detail: a texture, a door, a flower, a plate — the things a wide frame swallows. And the scale shot: a person, a bicycle, a boat against the landscape, so the viewer feels how big it all is. A place told in three different framings tells a fuller story than fifty near-identical wides.

**Working a viewpoint**

When a viewpoint works, don't take one frame and walk. Work it: wider and tighter, higher and lower, subject centered and off, landscape orientation and portrait. Small repositions move backgrounds and merge or separate overlaps — the intermediate skill is noticing which move improved what, and remembering it for the next location.

**Demonstration: one viewpoint, three framings**

One harbor scene, one tripod position. Frame one: the wide establishing view — water, boats, town behind. Frame two: the detail — ropes and hull paint, shot tight, shot at the same light. Frame three: scale — a person walking the dock, small against the masts. Three frames, one location, a complete sentence about the place.

**Packing light, shooting much**

Gear is an option, not a requirement. One body and one general-purpose zoom covers most travel; a wide prime or a long lens earn their carry only when your subject asks for them. The heaviest piece of equipment is the shot you didn't plan for.

**Learner practice**

Photograph a nearby place in three ways: a wide view, a detail, and an image that shows human activity or scale. Review the set as a story: does it say what the place felt like?

**Recap**

Light makes the appointment. Collect wide, detail, and scale. Work the viewpoint instead of taking one frame, and pack according to the subject, not the anxiety. A place is many photographs.

## 6. LEARNER PRACTICE

Photograph a nearby place — a street, park, or harbor — in three ways:

1. Wide: the establishing view, with a deliberate foreground anchor.
2. Detail: something small that the wide frame would have swallowed.
3. Scale: a person, bicycle, or vehicle against the scene.

Review the three as a set: does the story hold together? Note what you would shoot differently at a different hour.

If you're using a phone: the three-shot habit is the perfect phone discipline — the wide, the detail, and the scale are all about seeing, and the phone's pocketability is its own advantage.

## 7. RECAP

Light makes the appointment — scout it. Collect wide, detail, and scale at every location. Work the viewpoint instead of taking one frame. Pack for the subject, not for anxiety. A place is many photographs.
