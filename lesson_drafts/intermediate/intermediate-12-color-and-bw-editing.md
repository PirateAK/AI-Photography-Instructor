# Intermediate Lesson 12 — Color and Black-and-White Editing

Class: Intermediate

## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT

**Welcome**

Every photograph is edited — if only by the choices the camera made. This lesson is about the deliberate choice between two languages: color, and black and white. Same photograph, different statements.

**Color: relationships and mood**

In color, the photograph's feeling rides on relationships: warm against cool, complementary against clashing, saturated against muted. Small changes travel far — a shift in white balance that no one would call a "cast" can move the whole mood of a frame from brisk to nostalgic. Edit color with the same restraint as brightness: adjust relationships (warm this, calm that) rather than cranking saturation, which is the fastest route to unnatural.

**Black and white: weight without hue**

Remove color and the photograph must speak in brightness, texture, and shape alone. This is why a black-and-white conversion is a translation, not a filter: colors that looked distinct in color can collapse into the same gray, while a dull-colored corner becomes a luminous highlight when its channel is lifted. Good black-and-white work chooses tones deliberately — darks that hold, a clean white or two, and separation between subject and background earned by contrast, not hue.

**Demonstration: one photo, two treatments**

One photograph: a weathered door with a red frame against a gray wall. In color, the red frame is the story — the eye goes straight to it, and the mood is documentary and immediate. In black and white, the red becomes a mid-gray and the story moves: now it's the door's texture and the wall's tonal gradient, and the mood turns timeless. Same file, two purposes. In the simulator, you can preview this honestly — the mono picture style renders the conversion while you watch, and the histogram shows the tonal translation.

**Choosing per photo**

Choose the treatment by asking what the photograph is about. If the color IS the subject — a market's reds, autumn's gold — keep color. If the color is a distraction, or the picture's power is in shape, texture, and light, translate it. Write the purpose in a sentence; if you can't, the choice wasn't deliberate yet.

**Learner practice**

Make both versions of one photograph — color and black and white — and write one sentence about the purpose of each treatment. No sentence, no deliberate choice yet.

**Recap**

Color speaks in relationships and mood; black and white speaks in tonal weight and shape. Choose the language that serves what the photograph is about — and be able to say the purpose in one sentence.

## 6. LEARNER PRACTICE

Choose one photograph with a strong color element and make both versions:

1. Color version: adjust relationships — warmth, saturation, contrast — with restraint.
2. Black-and-white version: translate the tones deliberately; check that the subject separates from the background.
3. Write one sentence stating the purpose of each treatment.

If you're using a phone: preview the conversion in the simulator's mono picture style first — then apply the same intent in your editing app. The judgment transfers; the sliders differ.

## 7. RECAP

Color edits relationships; black and white edits tonal weight. A conversion is a translation, not a filter — hues collapse, textures rise. Choose the language that serves the photograph's purpose, and say that purpose in one sentence.
