# Motion, Blur & Freeze Lesson 1 - Where freeze ends

## 1. LEARNING OUTCOME
By the end of this 6-minute lesson, the student can identify their personal handheld shake floor and subject motion threshold using manual shutter speeds, and apply a reliable safety margin to freeze everyday action without unnecessary blur.

## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT
Welcome back, and thanks for joining me. Today we tackle one of the most common frustrations in photography: the blurry shot that looked totally fine on your tiny camera screen until you opened it up on your computer. I promise you, by the end of this lesson, you will stop guessing with your shutter speed and start controlling motion with total confidence. 

Let's start with a simple truth. Every single moving subject—and that includes your own hands holding the camera—has a critical shutter threshold where sharp details suddenly turn into an ugly smear. It is not a flaw in your gear. It is just physics. Your camera's shutter is simply a curtain that opens to let light hit the sensor. If something moves across that sensor while the curtain is open, it leaves a streak. That streak is motion blur.

Now, blur usually comes from one of two culprits. Culprit number one is camera shake. That is the micro-wobble in your hands when you press the shutter button. Culprit number two is subject motion. That is your kid running across the room, a dog chasing a ball, or even just a friend walking down the sidewalk. 

Let’s look at how this actually plays out on screen using our app's smear simulator. Imagine we are out on a bright Alaskan afternoon, and we have our camera set to Manual mode. We are currently shooting at a snappy shutter speed of 1/500 of a second. That means the shutter is open for just a fraction of a blink. Look at that simulator image right now: crisp edges, sharp texture on the jacket, completely frozen. 

Now, let's drop our shutter speed down. We will dial it through 1/250, down to 1/125, and right here to 1/60 of a second. Watch what happens to the walking subject on your screen. At 1/60, a subtle smear creeps across the frame. This magical, or rather frustrating, line right around 1/60 is your classic handheld shake floor. It is also the exact motion line where a casual walking subject starts to blur. If you hold your breath and brace your elbows against your ribs, you might squeak out a sharp shot at 1/60. But the moment your subject takes a normal stride, their legs and arms stretch into ghostly streaks. 

Let's keep going down just to see how bad it gets. We are scrolling down to 1/15 of a second, then 1/4 of a second, and finally a full 1-second exposure. Look at that simulator view now. The background is pinned sharp because our virtual camera is locked to a tripod, but our walking subject is practically invisible—just a ghostly streak of light moving across the frame. That is full subject smear. 

So why does this matter to you? Because knowing where your freeze ends lets you build a safety margin. If the baseline where blur starts for a walking person is 1/60 of a second, you never want to shoot at 1/60 when you actually need a sharp photo. You give yourself room to breathe. You bump your shutter speed up to 1/125 or 1/250 of a second. That extra cushion absorbs any unexpected movement and keeps your keeper rate high. 

Remember, seeing this blur happen in the simulator is your first step to mastering it. You are not failing when you get blur; you are just learning where your camera's limits live. In our next lesson, we will push past these limits into fast action sports and wildlife. For now, take a deep breath, keep practicing with your dial, and I will see you in the next module.

## 6. LEARNER PRACTICE
Open your camera or the app's simulator and find a subject that is walking or moving at a casual pace. Walk your shutter speed down step-by-step from 1/60 of a second all the way down to 1 full second, then back up to 1/500. Notice how the smear kicks in right around 1/60 — this is your handheld shake floor and your walking subject motion line. Pay attention to how fast the image degrades once you cross that threshold.

## 7. RECAP
- Every subject and camera setup has a shutter threshold where sharp details turn into motion blur.
- Blur comes from two main sources: micro-shake from your hands and movement from your subject.
- The 1/60 of a second mark is your critical baseline: the handheld shake floor and the walking subject motion line.
- Always build a safety margin by shooting one to two stops faster than your minimum threshold to guarantee sharp photos.
