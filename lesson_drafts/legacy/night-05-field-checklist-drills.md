## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT
Welcome back to our final session on night and celestial photography. Today, we bring everything together. We are putting our gear, our settings, and our habits into one smooth, repeatable workflow so you can walk into the dark with complete confidence.

Let's be honest. We have all driven an hour out to a dark sky location, set up our tripod in the freezing wind, and realized our battery is sitting on the kitchen counter. Or worse, we take our first test shot, zoom in on the LCD screen, and find a blurry mess of glowing star streaks because we bumped our focus ring. That sinking feeling is entirely preventable. And today, we are going to fix it for good with a foolproof pre-dark checklist.

Step one happens before the sun even sets: power and storage. Always pack at least two fully charged batteries—cold weather drains them fast—and format your memory card inside the camera before you leave home. 

Step two is focus. Never trust autofocus in the dark. Switch your lens to manual focus, find a distant light source or bright star, and use your camera's live view zoomed in to 10 times magnification to dial in pin-sharp stars. Once it is sharp, tape that focus ring down with gaffer tape so it cannot budge.

Step three is mastering the live histogram. That little graph of light and shadow isn't just for daytime shooters. When you take a test shot at ISO 3200, f/2.8, and 15 seconds, check that histogram. You want your brightness data pushed slightly off the left wall—meaning you captured the dark sky—without clipping the highlights of any bright stars. If the graph is jammed hard against the left edge, bump your ISO up one stop to 6400 or add a few seconds to your shutter speed. 

Now, how do we turn these skills into muscle memory? We use a structured practice plan. Here is your final drill: park your car, set up your tripod, and run our standard night starting recipe from scratch in total darkness. Set your aperture to your lens's widest setting, like f/2.8. Calculate your shutter speed using the 500 Rule—so 20 seconds for a 24mm lens. And dial your ISO to your tested ceiling, say 3200. 

Once your settings are punched in, verify each leg of that exposure triangle against your physical checklist. Aperture wide? Yes. Shutter at the rule? Yes. ISO at your ceiling? Yes. Take that shot, check your live histogram, and celebrate. You did it. 

You now have the tools, the settings, and the mindset to capture the night sky anywhere in the world. Thank you for joining me, and keep shooting.

## 6. LEARNER PRACTICE
Run the full night starting recipe, then verify each leg of the triangle against the checklist: aperture wide, shutter at the rule, ISO at your tested ceiling. Take one test shot, inspect your live histogram to ensure your shadows aren't crushed against the left wall, and adjust if needed before heading home.

## 7. RECAP
- Always complete your gear prep, battery check, and card formatting before leaving home.
- Lock your focus ring down with gaffer tape in daylight or twilight so you never lose infinity focus in the dark.
- Use the live histogram to verify your exposure data before committing to a long sequence of shots.
- Run through a repeatable, step-by-step physical checklist every single time you set up your tripod in the field.
