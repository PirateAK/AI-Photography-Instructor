# Portrait Photography Lesson 4 - Directing people who hate photos

## 1. LEARNING OUTCOME
By the end of this lesson, the student can guide an uncooperative or camera-shy subject using movement prompts and in-between moment timing, applying manual settings of 1/320s shutter speed and burst mode to capture natural, relaxed portraits.

## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT
Welcome back. Today we are tackling the ultimate portrait challenge: directing people who genuinely hate having their photo taken. I promise, by the end of this lesson, you will stop relying on awkward poses and start capturing real human connection. 

Think about the last time a friend or family member stepped in front of your lens, stiffened up like a board, and muttered, "I hate photos, I look terrible." It is not their fault. Most people freeze because they do not know what to do with their hands, and they hate the pressure of a looming lens. As the photographer, your job is not to bark out stiff posture commands. Your job is to create an environment where they forget the camera is even there. Let us fix that right now.

First, let us banish the word "pose" from your vocabulary. When you tell someone to "pose," their brain locks up. They overthink their posture, their smile turns into an uncomfortable grimace, and every muscle in their face goes rigid. Instead, we use prompts. Prompts are action-based instructions that require your subject to do something specific, which naturally results in authentic body language. 

For example, instead of saying, "Turn your head slightly and smile," try saying, "Look down at your shoes, and on the count of three, look up and tell me your favorite breakfast food." See what happens there? Their brain is suddenly busy remembering what they ate this morning, the micro-tension in their forehead melts away, and you get a genuine, thinking expression instead of a fake grin. 

Next up: movement exercises. Motion is your best friend when working with camera-shy people. When people are moving, they cannot overthink their posture. It loosens the joints, straightens the spine naturally, and brings life back into the eyes. 

Take your subject for a walk. Do not stop and shoot yet. Just chat while walking together. Then, give them a simple movement prompt: "Take three big steps toward me like you are late for a coffee date." Or try, "Spin around once and catch your balance." Because their body is occupied with executing the movement, their nervous system stops sounding the alarm bells about the camera. You are no longer taking a static mugshot; you are documenting a living, breathing moment. 

Now, let us talk about the magic zone: the in-between moments. The absolute best expression in any portrait almost always lives between the poses. It happens right after you lower the camera. It happens during the exhale, when they let out a breath and say, "Is that good?" It happens during the half-second laugh right after a bad joke. 

Most beginner photographers make the mistake of only clicking the shutter when they think the subject is "ready." But ready usually means stiff. You need to keep your camera up, keep shooting through the awkwardness, and keep shooting after the prompt is finished. The magic is in the reset. When they think the pressure is off, their shoulders drop two inches. That is your frame. That is the shot they will actually print and hang on their wall.

To catch these fleeting, fast-moving moments, your camera settings need to be locked in and ready for action. You cannot afford a blurry image when someone is walking, laughing, or spinning. Let us dial in our simulator right now. 

Set your shutter speed to 1/320th of a second. One-three-twentieth means your shutter is moving fast enough to freeze sudden head turns and light laughter without introducing motion blur. Pair that with a wide aperture like f/2.8 to keep your subject separated from the background, and let your ISO float to handle the lighting conditions. Finally, switch your drive mode from single shot to continuous high-speed, often called burst mode. When your subject bursts into a laugh, you want to hold down that shutter button and capture three or four frames in a second, ensuring you catch the exact peak of their expression. 

Camera confidence also transfers directly to your subject. If you are hunched behind your camera, whispering timidly, your subject will feel your nervous energy and tense right up. Stand tall. Lower the camera away from your face every few minutes to talk like a human being. Tell a terrible joke. Ask them a real question about their day. When you project calm, relaxed confidence, your subject borrows it from you. 

Let us review your practice drill for today. Grab a willing or semi-willing friend. Set your camera to manual, lock your shutter speed at 1/320s, and turn on burst mode. Give them three movement prompts—like walking toward you, looking away and turning back, or sharing a quick story—and shoot continuously through the awkwardness right into the in-between laughs. Do not stop shooting until they laugh at you. 

You now have the tools to turn camera-haters into confident portrait subjects. Go try it out, have fun with it, and I will see you in the next lesson.

## 6. LEARNER PRACTICE
Grab a friend or family member for a 10-minute mini session. Set your camera to manual with a shutter speed of 1/320s and turn on continuous burst mode. Skip traditional posing entirely; instead, give them three movement prompts (such as walking toward you or spinning around to face you) and shoot continuously through the awkwardness into the in-between laughs. Review your images afterward to find the frame where the natural laugh or exhale happened.

## 7. RECAP
- **Ditch the pose:** Replace rigid, stressful pose instructions with action-based prompts to relax your subject's mind and body.
- **Embrace movement:** Use walking, turning, and motion exercises to naturally eliminate stiffness and awkward posture.
- **Shoot the in-between:** The best expressions live right after the prompt ends—keep your camera up through the exhale and the post-laugh reset.
- **Lock your settings:** Use 1/320s shutter speed and continuous burst mode to freeze laughter-in-motion without motion blur.
- **Lead with confidence:** Your calm, conversational energy directly transfers to your subject, melting away their camera shyness.
