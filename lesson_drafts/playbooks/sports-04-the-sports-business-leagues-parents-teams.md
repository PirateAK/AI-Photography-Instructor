## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT
Welcome back to the Sports Playbook. Today, we are stepping off the sideline and into the office, because making a living in sports photography is about business. If you have mastered freezing fast action at 1/1600th of a second and nailing your exposures under Friday night lights, you already have the technical skills. Now, we are going to monetize them. 

Let us start with a reality check. Sports photography isn't just about one epic hero shot of a diving catch. That is your portfolio piece. The actual bread and butter of this business is volume. It is league picture days, parent portrait sales, weekend tournament packages, and working with local athletic brands. If you want to turn your passion into a sustainable business, you need to understand how these four markets operate, and more importantly, how to price and deliver for them.

Market number one: league and team contracts. This is your anchor work. Every spring, summer, and fall, thousands of youth soccer, baseball, football, and hockey leagues need team photos and individual player cards. A league contract means guaranteed volume. You shoot twenty teams in a weekend, process the images, and sell packages to parents. 

Market number two: parent portraits. Parents want professional memories of their kids, full stop. But the key here is speed and convenience. If you make ordering easy through an online gallery, and if your print quality is miles ahead of what parents can snap on their cell phones, you will create a reliable revenue stream that funds your gear upgrades for years.

Market number three: tournament coverage. Tournaments are marathons. You are covering dozens of games across multiple fields over a single weekend. You sell action shots directly to parents and players, often on-site with a mobile printing station or via a digital download link sent right to their phones before they even drive home.

Market number four: athletic brands and local high schools. This is where your artistic sports work shines. Brands need dynamic, high-contrast images for social media, banners, and recruiting materials. This work is usually billed at a day rate plus licensing fees, rather than volume portrait sales.

Now, how do you handle the volume without burning out? The secret is workflow consistency. When you are shooting a league picture day with two hundred kids, you cannot afford to mess up your camera settings. You lock your camera in manual mode. You set your shutter speed to 1/250th of a second to sync cleanly with your strobe or flash, your aperture to f/5.6 to get both the front and back rows of a team sharp, and your ISO to 100 for maximum clarity. You use a custom white balance preset so skin tones match across every single image. You shoot, you check your histogram, and you keep moving. No fiddling, no guesswork.

This brings us to pricing, which ties right back into the lessons you learned in our Pro class. Never undervalue your time. When pricing a league contract, you can use a zero-upfront-fee model where you make your money purely on print and digital package sales, or you charge a team media fee to guarantee your day rate, plus commission on sales. Always build tiered packages. Offer a basic digital file package, a mid-tier print package with memory mates—which combine the team photo and the individual player shot into one custom template—and a premium package with trading cards and stat prints. 

Turnaround time is your currency. In the modern sports market, speed wins repeat contracts. If you promise a 48-hour turnaround on a weekend tournament or a league picture day, you have to hit it. That means your culling and editing workflow must be bulletproof. You use batch editing in Lightroom, apply your signature sports contrast preset, export jpegs sized perfectly for mobile viewing, and upload them to your gallery software while you eat dinner. 

To help you land this work, you need a professional coverage proposal. A winning proposal is simple, clear, and professional. It includes a cover letter, an overview of your deliverables per team—like one high-res team composite and individual player portraits—your per-player portrait options, and your ironclad 48-hour delivery promise. You outline your pricing clearly so the league president doesn't have to guess what things cost.

For your lesson practice today, you are going to draft that exact league coverage proposal. Outline your deliverables, set your portrait options using your Pro pricing structure, and commit to that 48-hour turnaround. Then, go out, shoot one practice session with a local team or group of kids, and deliver a polished, five-image sample set on that exact 48-hour clock. 

You have the technical skills. Now put the business framework around them, build your volume workflow, and go run a profitable sports photography business. We will see you out there.

## 8. ACCURACY CHECK
- Question: Why is a manual exposure setting (like ISO 100, f/5.6, 1/250s) critical for league picture days?
- Answer: It ensures absolute consistency in lighting, depth of field, and image quality across dozens or hundreds of different subjects, drastically reducing editing time.
- Question: What is a "memory mate" in sports league photography?
- Answer: A custom-designed print template that combines a group team photo with an individual portrait of the player on a single sheet.
- Question: Why is a strict turnaround promise (such as 48 hours) vital for sports business success?
- Answer: Parents and league organizers expect fast results, and delivering quickly builds trust, drives immediate sales, and secures repeat contracts for the following season.
