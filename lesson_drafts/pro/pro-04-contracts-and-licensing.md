## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT

**Welcome**

The brief says what happens; the agreement says what happens when something ELSE happens. A written agreement protects both parties — the client gets the deliverables defined, you get the scope, payment, and credit defined. This lesson walks the vocabulary and the questions. One thing it is not: legal advice. Agreement language varies by jurisdiction, and this lesson's repeated instruction is to raise specifics with a qualified professional.

**What a written agreement does**

The agreement anchors five things to the brief: scope (the deliverables, from the estimate), payment (amounts, schedule, late terms), usage rights (who may use the images, where, for how long), responsibilities (cancellation, weather, reshoots, safety), and resolution (what happens in a dispute). When any of the five is missing, that's where trouble breeds.

**Usage rights: the vocabulary**

Usage is described by four dimensions. WHO uses the images — the client only, or their agencies? WHERE — web, social, print, broadcast, out-of-home? FOR HOW LONG — one year, five years, perpetuity? And EXCLUSIVITY — may you license the same images to others, or is this usage exclusive? Broader, longer, and exclusive all cost more — the pricing lesson's licensing line expands here into the agreement's license clause.

**Walking a sample agreement**

A sample one-page agreement, section by section: the parties and the date; the brief referenced as the scope; deliverables listed as count, format, and delivery method; the license clause — who, where, how long, exclusive or not; payment schedule; cancellation and rescheduling terms; weather and force-majeure handling for outdoor work; a credit clause if agreed; and the governing-law note — the line that reminds you which jurisdiction's rules apply. Read it as two people: as the photographer, and as the client. Anything ambiguous from either seat is a question.

**The questions to raise — and with whom**

Raise with a QUALIFIED PROFESSIONAL — a lawyer or qualified advisor in your jurisdiction — before you need them: your standard template's validity, copyright registration practice where relevant, model and property release requirements for your market, and any clause you don't fully understand. Raise with the CLIENT, in plain language: usage scope, delivery schedule, approval process, and what happens if the brief changes. Different audiences, different questions — never blur them.

**When the client sends THEIR agreement**

Larger clients send their own paper. Read the license clause first: usage they're granting themselves, exclusivity, credit removal. Mark everything you don't understand and take it to your qualified professional before signing. Negotiating a clause isn't hostility — it's professionalism.

**Learner practice**

Walk the sample agreement twice — once as photographer, once as client — and list every term you'd question, plus the two questions you'd take to a qualified professional.

**Recap**

Written agreements anchor scope, payment, usage, responsibilities, and resolution. Usage rights have four dimensions — who, where, how long, exclusive. Walk every agreement from both seats, raise legal specifics with a qualified professional, and never sign what you don't understand.

5. Compare your lists with a working photographer's if you know one; note what they flagged that you missed.
