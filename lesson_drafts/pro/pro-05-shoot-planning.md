# Pro Lesson 5 — Professional Shoot Planning

Class: Pro
Prerequisite: Pro Lessons 2 and 4

## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT

**Welcome**

Planning is shooting that happens early. The approved brief already contains the shoot — this lesson extracts it: a shot list, a schedule, a location plan, an equipment list, and contingencies, assembled the day before so shoot-day has one job.

**The shot list: translating the brief**

Every deliverable in the brief becomes a shot-list line. The bakery brief promised around twenty web images — the list says what they are: the storefront, the counter, the ovens at work, the baker's hands, six product frames, the exterior with a customer. Ten to fifteen planned lines expand naturally to twenty delivered images. The list is a floor, not a cage — but without it, you're guessing what the client approved.

**Schedule and location plan**

Work backwards from the light and the business. What time is the light right? When does the shop get busy? When is the baker free? The schedule sequences the shot list around those constraints — products first while the counter's clean, the exterior at the good hour, the people shots when the rush ends. The location plan notes where you'll stand, where the subject stands, where the light comes from at that hour, and where gear lives.

**Equipment list: driven by the shot list**

Read the shot list line by line and pull equipment forward: product frames need the tripod and the 50mm; the exterior needs the wider lens; the hands shot needs a fast aperture. Batteries charged, cards formatted and packed, and the boring items — the ones that end shoots — on the list because they're the ones forgotten.

**Contingencies: the plan for the plan**

Every plan has a failure surface. Rain for the exterior — what's the indoor alternative frame? The oven breaks — what else tells "bakery"? The client's late — which shots don't need them? One contingency line per likely failure, written down. You're not predicting; you're pre-deciding.

**Demonstration: the bakery checklist built**

The bakery brief, live. Shot list: twelve lines from the twenty-image promise. Schedule: products at open, people mid-morning, exterior at golden hour. Location plan: counter position mapped, window light charted. Equipment list pulled line by line — two lenses, tripod, batteries, cards, and a cloth for the display case. Contingencies: rain alternative, oven alternative, late-client alternative. One page. Shoot day now has one job: execute.

**Learner practice**

Build the full pre-shoot checklist for the bakery assignment — or a real brief of yours — and give it to someone to find the holes. They always find holes.

**Recap**

The brief contains the shoot; planning extracts it. Shot list from the deliverables, schedule backwards from light and business, location mapped, equipment pulled line by line, and one written contingency per likely failure. Shoot day's job: execute.

## 6. LEARNER PRACTICE

Build the full pre-shoot checklist for a real or invented brief:

1. Shot list — one line per deliverable group.
2. Schedule — worked backwards from light and availability.
3. Location plan — positions, light direction, gear staging.
4. Equipment list — pulled shot-list line by line, plus the boring items.
5. Contingency — one written line per likely failure.

Hand the checklist to a friend or colleague and ask them to break it. Add what they find.

## 7. RECAP

The brief contains the shoot. Extract it: shot list from the deliverables, schedule backwards from the light, location mapped, equipment pulled from the list, contingencies pre-decided in writing. Planning is shooting that happens early — and shoot day becomes execution instead of improvisation.

## 8. ACCURACY CHECK

- The demonstration checklist is one example for one brief; structures vary by project — present the extraction method as the transferable part.
- Golden-hour and timing claims depend on season and latitude; the demo uses its own dates.
- Contingency lists are examples, not complete risk registers — the risk lesson (Pro 12) formalizes this further.
- Verify the checklist text is legible on camera and matches the narration line by line.
