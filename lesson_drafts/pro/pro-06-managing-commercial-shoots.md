# Pro Lesson 6 — Managing a Commercial Shoot

Class: Pro
Prerequisite: Pro Lesson 5

## 1. LEARNING OUTCOME

By the end of this lesson, learners can coordinate people, time, lighting, and deliverables on a working shoot — and adapt in the moment when delays or changes hit the schedule.

## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT

**Welcome**

The checklist was built for calm. Shoot day tests it against reality — late arrivals, changed light, a client with new ideas. Managing a commercial shoot is running the plan while adapting to the day, in that order.

**Opening the shoot: the briefing and the rhythm**

Arrive early enough that setup doesn't eat the schedule. Open with the briefing: today's shot list in order, the schedule's anchors, who approves what, where gear lives. Give the subject or contact one clear role — "you're the hands in the oven shots." Then find the rhythm: the checklist in your pocket or with an assistant, worked in order so nothing promised is missed.

**Working the list without being owned by it**

The shot list is the floor, not a cage — the Intermediate planning lesson said so, and shoot day proves it. Work the list in order, but when a better frame appears — a moment, a light change, a happy accident — take it, then return to the list. The discipline is returning. The client remembers the frames they asked for; the inspired frames are the bonus, not the substitute.

**The client on set: expectations in real time**

When the client attends, manage the relationship, not just the camera. Show them frames periodically — it builds confidence and catches misunderstandings while they're cheap. Translate their ideas into the shot list graciously: "great — let's add that after the counter series." If a new idea conflicts with the schedule, say so with the timeline in hand, and let them choose what slips. The alternative — silent resentment or silent overtime — is how shoots end badly.

**When it goes sideways: delays and changes**

Something will go sideways: the baker's late, the light moved, the oven's broken. The contingencies you pre-decided now pay off — run the alternative without drama. Re-sequence the shot list to fit the delay: shoot what's available now, push what needs the missing piece. Communicate the change to the client in one sentence — "the oven's down, so we're doing the counter set first; the oven shots after lunch" — and keep moving. Adaptation announced is professionalism; adaptation silent is anxiety.

**Closing the shoot: the wrap habit**

Before packing: check the shot list — every promised frame present? Cards verified, batteries counted, gear inventoried against the packing list. Thank the people who worked it. And confirm the next step out loud: "selection gallery to you by Thursday." The wrap habit is how a professional leaves a shoot — complete, verified, and with the client already knowing what happens next.

**Learner practice**

Run a real shoot — any scale — from your checklist, including one deliberate adaptation, and close it with the wrap habit. Write down what went sideways and which pre-decided contingency caught it.

**Recap**

Brief the day, work the list in order, take the bonus frames but always return. Manage the client in real time — show frames, translate ideas, surface trade-offs. Adapt aloud, using pre-decided contingencies. And close with the wrap habit: list checked, gear verified, next step promised.

## 6. LEARNER PRACTICE

Run one shoot from your Lesson 5 checklist at any scale:

1. Brief everyone present: list order, schedule anchors, one role each.
2. Work the list in order; take at least one bonus frame and return to the list.
3. Surface one client trade-off aloud with the timeline in hand (or simulate it).
4. Hit one contingency — even a small one — and announce it.
5. Close with the wrap habit: list verified, gear counted, next step promised.

Write down what went sideways and which pre-decision caught it.

## 7. RECAP

Brief the day and find the rhythm. Work the list in order — bonus frames welcome, always return. Manage the client in real time: show frames, translate ideas, surface trade-offs. Adapt aloud with pre-decided contingencies. Close with the wrap habit: complete, verified, next step promised.
