## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT

**Welcome**

The shoot's over; the project isn't. Delivery is part of the product — clients remember the handoff as vividly as the photographs. This lesson covers proofing, selections, delivery with the agreed license, and the records that close the folder cleanly.

**Proofing: presenting the candidates**

Proofing shows the EDITED-ENOUGH-TO-JUDGE candidates — color-corrected, presentable, not yet individually finished. Present them in a gallery the client can mark: a proofing service, a private web gallery, or a clean PDF for smaller jobs. The presentation shapes the selection: order matters, and proofing only your strongest candidates focuses the client's choices. Proof everything and clients edit everything.

**Gathering selections without the edit-all loop**

State the selection rules in writing with the gallery: how many images the brief covers, the deadline, and what happens after — selections close, finishing begins. Without a deadline, selections drift for weeks and the edit-all loop begins: "one more tweak" until the project dies of niceness. The deadline is a kindness to both sides.

**Delivery: formats, licensing, and the handoff**

Deliver what the brief and license define: the count, the sizes (print-resolution where agreed, web-sized copies where practical), in folders that name themselves — web, print, social. Include the license summary in writing: what the client may do with these images, matching the agreement. And the handoff note: what was delivered, in what format, where it lives. A delivery without a summary invites a month of "can you resend number six?"

**Demonstration: bakery proof to final files**

The bakery project, end to end. The proof gallery: twenty-two edited-enough candidates, ordered — exterior first, products grouped, people together — with the written rules: "choose the twenty the brief covers, by Friday." The client marks twenty-two and asks for two more; the brief's number is confirmed, the extras priced per the estimate's scope line, and the final twenty set. Delivery: web-sized folder, print folder, license summary, handoff note. Project records: brief, estimate, agreement, proof gallery, selections, delivered files — one folder, closed.

**Project records: closing the folder**

The records are the next project's head start: what was delivered, when, under what license, at what price. When the bakery calls next spring, the folder answers every question before it's asked.

**Learner practice**

Run a real or simulated delivery: proof a set with written rules, gather selections against a deadline, deliver the agreed files with a license summary and handoff note, and close the project folder with records complete.

**Recap**

Proof edited-enough candidates with written selection rules and a deadline. Deliver exactly what the brief and license define — sizes, folders, license summary, handoff note. Close the project folder with complete records. Delivery is part of the product.

## 6. LEARNER PRACTICE

Run one delivery end to end:

1. Proof a set: edited-enough candidates, ordered, presented in a gallery or PDF.
2. Write the selection rules: count, deadline, and what happens after.
3. Gather selections; handle any over-selection using the estimate's scope line.
4. Deliver: folders that name themselves, sizes as agreed, license summary in writing.
5. Close the project folder: brief, estimate, agreement, proofs, selections, delivered files — all findable in one minute.

## 7. RECAP

Proof edited-enough candidates — your strongest, ordered, with written selection rules and a deadline. Deliver exactly the brief and license define, in folders that name themselves, with the handoff note. Close the project folder with complete records: the next project's head start.
