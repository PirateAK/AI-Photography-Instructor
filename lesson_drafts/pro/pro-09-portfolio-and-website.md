# Pro Lesson 9 — Portfolio and Website Development

Class: Pro
Prerequisite: Pro Lesson 8; Advanced Lesson 10

## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT

**Welcome**

A portfolio is not an archive of your best work — it's an ARGUMENT that you can do a specific job. Everything in it either supports that argument or dilutes it. This lesson builds the argument for a chosen audience.

**Audience first: who is this for?**

The niche statement from Pro Lesson 1 names the audience. Families hiring a portrait photographer read portfolios differently than a marketing manager hiring a product shooter — different images, different order, different words. One photographer can maintain two portfolios; one portfolio cannot serve two audiences equally. Choose the audience before choosing a single image.

**Selection: the niche's promise, proven**

The niche promised a kind of photograph; the portfolio must PROVE it with work. Prove depth, not luck: three to five strong images in the promised genre beat one perfect frame. Prove currency: recent work says you're active. And prove range within the niche — the same voice applied to different subjects and conditions shows reliability, which is what clients are actually buying. The Advanced selection lesson's two passes apply: ruthless first, criteria second — and the criterion here is the niche's promise.

**Order and presentation: the first five images**

Visitors decide in the first five images — the argument's opening. Lead with the strongest, follow with variety within the voice, and end with a call to action. Presentation details carry weight too: consistent crops, consistent editing across the set, no clashing frames, and text that says what you do in one line — the niche sentence, verbatim.

**Demonstration: one portfolio, two audiences**

One photographer, two arguments. For families: warm frames first, full-family moments, the session experience visible, pricing page friendly. For commercial clients: the product and interior work first, tighter crops, the process and reliability visible, estimate language professional. Same photographs reshuffled and re-cropped into two different arguments — the portfolio is the message, and the message serves the audience.

**The website minimums**

Whatever platform hosts it, a professional site needs: the portfolio set (proven, ordered), the niche sentence where a stranger lands, an about page with a real photograph of you, a contact path that works, and — for the app's users — a link to your classes or store. Load speed and mobile layout are part of the presentation: most first visits are phones.

**Learner practice**

Build or refresh a portfolio for ONE audience: select against the niche's promise, order for the first five images, and verify the message matches the niche sentence.

**Recap**

The portfolio is an argument for a specific job. Choose the audience first; prove the niche with depth, currency, and range; win the first five images; and keep the presentation consistent to the niche sentence. Two audiences, two portfolios — never one argument trying to be everything.

## 6. LEARNER PRACTICE

Build or refresh a portfolio for one audience:

1. Write the niche sentence and name the audience.
2. Select against the promise: depth, currency, range — the Advanced selection passes.
3. Order for the first five images; check the set's crops and editing consistency.
4. Verify the website minimums: niche sentence, about page with a real photo, working contact, mobile layout.
5. Show it to someone in the target audience and ask what work they'd hire you for. Their answer grades the argument.

## 7. RECAP

The portfolio is an argument for a specific job. Choose the audience first; prove the niche with depth, currency, and range; win the first five images with consistent presentation; and verify the site's five minimums. Two audiences means two arguments — never one portfolio apologizing to both.

## 8. ACCURACY CHECK

- "Visitors decide in five images" is a design convention, not a measured law; phrase as the working assumption.
- Platform claims (load speed, mobile behavior) must match the demonstrated site.
- Portfolio examples must be the photographer's actual work, or clearly labeled stock illustrations.
- Do not present any portfolio platform as required; the minimums are platform-independent.
- Verify the niche sentence appears verbatim on the demonstrated site.
