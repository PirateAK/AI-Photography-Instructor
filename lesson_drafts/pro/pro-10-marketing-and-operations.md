## 3. NARRATION SCRIPT

**Welcome**

The photographs are the visible half of a photography business. The quiet half is operations: inquiries answered, estimates sent, schedules kept, invoices tracked, work promoted — the same steps every time. This lesson maps that path and shows where consistency saves the business.

**The inquiry-to-delivery map**

Every project walks the same path: inquiry → consultation → brief → estimate → agreement → planning → shoot → selection → delivery → follow-up. Ten stages, and each has a template or a checklist from the previous lessons — the consultation questions, the estimate structure, the pre-shoot checklist, the wrap habit, the delivery summary. Operations means every inquiry walks the whole map, in order, every time — because the steps skipped under pressure are the ones that fail.

**Promotion that fits a niche**

Promotion for a niche business is steady presence, not campaigns. The portfolio stays current — new strong work replaces old, quarterly. The niche's channels get fed: the places your five named clients actually look — local search, community boards, the platform your genre lives on. And referrals are asked for, politely and specifically, at delivery: "if you know anyone who needs this kind of work, I'd be grateful." One sentence, said at the moment of maximum goodwill.

**Tracking: the board that holds the truth**

A simple tracker — a spreadsheet or a board with columns — holds the truth of the business: inquiries in, briefs sent, estimates pending, agreements signed, shoots scheduled, deliveries made, invoices paid. Updated after every stage. The board answers the three questions that otherwise live in anxiety: what's pending, what's owed to me, and what needs a nudge today.

**Demonstration: one inquiry through the map**

The bakery's second inquiry — "spring menu refresh?" — walks the board. Inquiry logged. Consultation booked; brief updated from last year's. Estimate itemized and sent; agreement returned. Planning checklist reused from the first shoot — half the lines already filled. Shoot day executes; delivery folder repeats its structure; follow-up asks for referrals. Every stage had a template. The total admin time: noticeably less than the first engagement — that's operations paying for itself.

**The between-shoots habit**

Between shoots, the business lives on small consistent acts: the portfolio refresh, the referral ask, the board updated, one piece of outreach to the niche's channels. An hour a week of operations keeps the pipeline from being the thing that ends the business.

**Learner practice**

Draw your own inquiry-to-delivery map with the templates you have, build the tracking board, and run one real or simulated inquiry through every stage — updating the board at each step.

**Recap**

Every project walks the map: inquiry, consultation, brief, estimate, agreement, planning, shoot, selection, delivery, follow-up — with a template at each stage. Promotion is steady presence in the niche's channels plus the referral ask. The tracking board holds the truth. An hour of operations a week keeps the pipeline alive.

## 6. LEARNER PRACTICE

Map and run your operations:

1. Draw your inquiry-to-delivery map — ten stages or your variant — and attach the template you have for each.
2. Build the tracking board: one column per stage; a card per inquiry.
3. Write your referral-ask sentence, and plan when you'll say it — delivery is the moment.
4. Run one real or simulated inquiry through every stage, updating the board each time.
5. Book the between-shoots hour in your calendar: portfolio refresh, one outreach, board updated.

## 7. RECAP

Ten stages, walked in order, every time — each with a template from the previous lessons. Promotion is steady presence: portfolio current, niche channels fed, referrals asked at delivery. The tracking board holds the truth — pending, owed, needs-a-nudge. An hour a week of operations keeps the pipeline alive.

- Referral-asking phrasing varies by culture; present the timing (delivery, maximum goodwill) as the transferable part.
- No income or growth promises — operations improve consistency and communication, as the brief states.
